# How long should I keep client consultation and patch test records? (UK)

There is no single legal number, which is why salon owners get conflicting answers. Two separate pressures set the period, and they pull in opposite directions.

The first is the claims window. Under the Limitation Act 1980, someone has three years from the injury — or from the date they realised it was caused by a treatment — to bring a personal injury claim. "Date of knowledge" is the part that catches people out: a reaction blamed on something else for two years can still land on you later. Because of that, most beauty and hair insurers require records to be kept well beyond three years, commonly seven. Your policy wording is the binding number, not a rule of thumb, so check it and write that figure down.

For clients treated under 18, the clock does not start until their eighteenth birthday, so a claim can arrive until they are 21. Keep minors' records longer than adults'.

The second pressure is UK GDPR. Consultation cards carry health information — special category data under Article 9 — and the storage limitation principle says you cannot keep it indefinitely "just in case." You must set a defined retention period, be able to justify it, state it in your privacy notice, and actually delete when it expires.

What that looks like in practice:

- Pick one period, match it to your insurer's requirement, and apply it consistently.
- Run retention from the client's **last** appointment, not their first.
- Keep patch test records to the same schedule — they are the evidence that matters most in a colour claim.
- Delete on time, and be able to show the deletion happened.

A drawer of cards nobody has opened in a decade fails both tests at once: unusable as evidence, and unlawful to hold.
