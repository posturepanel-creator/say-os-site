# Can I massage a client with fibromyalgia?

Yes. Fibromyalgia is not a contraindication to massage, and many clients find gentle treatment helpful. The risk is not harm to the condition itself but doing too much: pressure that suits most clients can leave a fibromyalgia client in a flare for days.

Why it matters: fibromyalgia causes widespread pain, heightened sensitivity to touch and pressure, fatigue, and poor sleep. Symptoms vary from day to day, so a treatment that was fine last month may not be fine today.

How to adapt:

- **Lighter pressure, slower pace.** Start well below your normal pressure and let the client guide you. Avoid deep tissue, sports massage, and vigorous techniques, and don't chase tender points.
- **Shorter sessions.** 30 to 45 minutes is often enough. Fatigue is part of the condition, and a long session can leave the client drained afterwards.
- **Mind the temperature.** Many clients are sensitive to cold and heat. Keep the room warm, use warm towels, and check before using hot stones or heated products.
- **Check in throughout.** Ask how they are today, not just at the start. Sensitivity changes, and clients don't always speak up mid-treatment.
- **Expect a delayed reaction.** Soreness can appear 24 to 48 hours later. Tell the client this, and use it to adjust the next visit.

Ask at consultation how their symptoms are right now, which areas are most sensitive, what has helped or hurt before, and whether they are on pain medication that could dull sensation. If they are in a bad flare, rebook rather than push on. Record their answers and re-check them at every visit, since a fibromyalgia client's baseline can shift week to week.
