# Can I treat a client who has just had a vaccine?

Usually yes. There is no UK rule that stops you treating a client after a flu, COVID-19 or other routine vaccination, but common sense and a good consultation matter more than a fixed waiting period.

Most reactions are mild and short-lived: a sore, warm or swollen arm, tiredness, a low temperature, aches, and sometimes tender lymph nodes in the armpit for a few days. That shapes what is sensible:

- **Avoid the injection site.** Skip massage, waxing, peels, microneedling or any device on the vaccinated upper arm for at least 24-48 hours, or until it is no longer tender.
- **Take care around the armpit.** Swollen nodes make underarm waxing and lymphatic or upper-body massage uncomfortable, so postpone those.
- **Treat feeling unwell like any other illness.** If the client has a fever, chills or flu-like symptoms, rebook, as you would for any [cold or flu](/blog/can-i-treat-a-client-with-a-cold-flu-or-covid-symptoms-uk).
- **Be cautious with heat and strong actives.** A client who feels run down may react more than usual, so patch test and keep intensity low.
- **Consider delaying injectables or deep treatments.** Some aesthetic practitioners wait a couple of weeks after a vaccine before dermal filler or other medical-grade treatments. Follow your own insurer's and training provider's advice here.

Always ask at consultation about recent vaccinations, including travel jabs, and note the date and site on the record card. If a client has a severe or unusual reaction, they should contact their GP or NHS 111. If you are unsure, check your insurer's guidance before going ahead.
