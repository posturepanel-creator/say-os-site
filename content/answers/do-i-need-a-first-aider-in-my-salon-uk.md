# Do I need a trained first aider in my salon?

Not always, but you do need a first-aid plan. Under the Health and Safety (First-Aid) Regulations 1981 (Great Britain), employers must provide adequate first-aid equipment, facilities and people. What counts as "adequate" comes from a short first-aid needs assessment, not a fixed rule.

For a typical low-risk salon, HSE guidance sets the minimum like this:

- **Fewer than 25 employees:** at least one **appointed person**. They take charge in an emergency, call 999 and keep the first-aid kit stocked. They are not required to hold a first-aid qualification.
- **25 to 50 employees:** at least one trained first aider, usually with an Emergency First Aid at Work (EFAW) certificate.

Your assessment may justify more. Salons handle hot wax, chemicals, blades and electrical equipment, and clients can faint or react to products mid-treatment. Many owners train at least one person to EFAW level anyway. Certificates last three years, and HSE recommends annual refresher sessions.

Other points to know:

- **Self-employed therapists and chair renters** are responsible for their own first-aid provision.
- **Clients are not covered by the regulations**, but HSE strongly recommends including them in your plan.
- Your insurer may require a trained first aider, so check your policy.
- Keep a stocked kit, record any treatment you give, and report qualifying injuries under [RIDDOR](https://www.hse.gov.uk/riddor/).

Northern Ireland has its own regulations, so check with HSENI there.
