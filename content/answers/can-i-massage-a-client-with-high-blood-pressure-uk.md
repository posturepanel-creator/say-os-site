# Can I do a massage on a client with high blood pressure?

Usually yes — but with conditions, and never blind. High blood pressure (hypertension) is common and mostly not a reason to refuse. The risk isn't the massage itself; it's not knowing the client's numbers, their medication, and whether the pressure is controlled.

Why it matters: deep, vigorous, or full-body massage increases circulation and can affect blood pressure during and after the treatment. For a client whose hypertension is uncontrolled, or who has related complications (heart disease, recent stroke, kidney problems), that shift carries real risk. Some blood-pressure medications also cause dizziness when the client stands up from the couch — a falls hazard you're responsible for.

What to do:

- **Ask, and record it.** Diagnosed hypertension? Controlled? On medication? Any related heart or circulatory conditions? Log the answers on the consultation form.
- **If controlled and stable,** a gentle-to-moderate massage is generally fine. Avoid deep tissue and prolonged heat.
- **If uncontrolled, undiagnosed but suspected, or recently changed medication,** don't proceed — signpost to their GP for clearance first.
- **Sit them up slowly** at the end and let them steady before standing, to avoid a dizzy drop.
- **Skip the sauna, hot stones, and heat wraps** for anyone with poorly controlled pressure.

Turning the treatment into a blood-pressure diagnosis isn't your job — but spotting the red flags and knowing when to pause is. If you're ever unsure, GP clearance protects the client and you.
