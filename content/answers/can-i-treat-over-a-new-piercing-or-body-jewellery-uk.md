# Can I treat over a new piercing or body jewellery?

Not over a piercing that is still healing. A new piercing is an open wound, and waxing, massage, peels, spray tan, laser or IPL on or around it risk infection, irritation, and delayed healing. Treat the surrounding area only once the piercing has fully settled.

Typical healing times vary, so ask the client when it was done rather than guessing:

- **Ear lobe:** around 6–8 weeks.
- **Cartilage (upper ear, nose):** 3–12 months.
- **Navel:** 6–12 months, which matters for bikini, abdomen and tanning treatments.
- **Nipple and genital piercings:** several months at minimum, so avoid the area entirely.

Signs a piercing is **not** ready: redness, swelling, heat, discharge, crusting, or tenderness. Any of these means no treatment nearby and a suggestion that the client sees their piercer or GP.

**Established piercings and jewellery** need a different check. Metal conducts heat, so for laser, IPL, radiofrequency and microcurrent the jewellery should come out of the treatment area or the area should be avoided. Where it can't be removed (or would close up), work around it with clear margins. For waxing and massage, keep a safe distance and never pull wax across jewellery.

Don't remove a client's jewellery yourself. Ask them to, and record that you did.

Write down the piercing, its age, what you advised, and the client's decision on their consultation record. That is health-related data under UK GDPR, so keep it in a secure client file rather than on paper notes.
