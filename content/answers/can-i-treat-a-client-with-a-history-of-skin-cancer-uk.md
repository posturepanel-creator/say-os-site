# Can I treat a client with a history of skin cancer?

Short answer: often yes, but the treatment, the site and the stage of recovery decide — and a doctor's sign-off is the safe default for anything that stimulates or exposes the skin.

Skin cancer (basal cell carcinoma, squamous cell carcinoma or melanoma) is not a blanket contraindication. What matters is where it was, how it was treated and whether the client is still under review. Ask at consultation, and record the answers.

- **Safe to go ahead, with care** — massage, manicures, pedicures, lash and brow work, and facials that don't involve active ingredients or devices, provided you stay clear of the affected area.
- **Never treat over** — an active lesion, a surgical or radiotherapy site, or a scar that hasn't fully healed. Skin treated with radiotherapy is fragile and can stay sensitised for years.
- **Get written medical clearance first** — laser, IPL, chemical peels, microneedling, radiofrequency and strong exfoliation. These add heat, trauma or photosensitivity, and can mask or mimic changes a doctor needs to see.
- **Waxing** — avoid the treated area and any skin that is thin, scarred or irritated.
- **Spray tan and sunbed-style treatments** — usually fine for a spray tan, but check with the client, and never advise them to use sunbeds.

Clients with a history of skin cancer are at higher risk of another, so you may spot something new. If a mole or patch is changing, bleeding, crusting or not healing, stop treating that area and advise a GP visit. Don't diagnose or reassure.

Check your insurer's wording too: many policies expect medical clearance for advanced treatments on clients with a cancer history.
