# Can I massage a client with DVT or a history of blood clots?

Suspected or active DVT is an absolute no — not "adapt your technique", not "work gently around it". Massage over or near a clot can dislodge it, and a dislodged clot can travel to the lungs (pulmonary embolism). This is one of the few contraindications in the treatment room with a genuinely life-threatening downside.

The practical standard:

- **Active or suspected DVT:** no massage, no body treatments on that limb, full stop. One swollen, warm, hard, or painful calf — especially one leg and not the other — means stop and send the client for urgent medical advice. Don't book them back in until they're cleared.
- **History of clots, now resolved:** ask for written confirmation from their GP or specialist before any deep leg work. Many clients on long-term anticoagulants are fine for adapted treatment, but that's the prescriber's call, not yours.
- **On anticoagulants:** lighter pressure only — bruising risk is real even when the clot risk has passed.
- **Higher-risk clients:** recent surgery, long-haul flights, pregnancy, and immobility all raise clot risk. A leg that looked fine at the last visit can be the red-flag leg today, so check every time, not just at the first consultation.
