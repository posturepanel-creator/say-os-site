# How do I reduce no-shows at my salon?

No-shows aren't random. They follow patterns, and most are preventable without charging anyone a penny upfront.

Start with reminders that actually land. A single booking confirmation isn't enough. Send one when the appointment is made, a second 48 hours before, and a third on the morning of. The 48-hour reminder is the one that matters most — it gives a client enough time to rebook rather than vanish, and it's far enough out that the slot can be filled.

Make rescheduling effortless. Most no-shows aren't people who didn't want to come — they're people who couldn't face the phone call to move it. If a client can reschedule themselves in one tap, they'll move the booking instead of ghosting it. A missed-but-moved appointment costs you nothing; a no-show costs you the whole slot.

Know who's likely to drop. New clients and first-time bookings no-show far more than regulars. For those, a deposit or a quick confirmation request is reasonable. For loyal clients, it would only insult them — treat the two groups differently.

Finally, keep a record. When the same name no-shows twice, that's information, not a coincidence. A system that quietly tracks reliability lets you protect your busiest slots without ever having an awkward conversation.
