# Do I need a licence for laser hair removal or IPL in the UK?

It depends heavily on where you trade, and laser/IPL sits in a different licensing lane to microneedling or semi-permanent makeup — don't assume one licence covers both.

**London boroughs.** You need a special treatment licence under the London Local Authorities Act 1991 before offering laser or IPL at all. Fees vary hugely by borough — from a few hundred pounds up to well over £1,000 for a new licence in the highest-risk tier. Councils typically want risk assessments, staff training records, a clearly defined controlled treatment area with warning signage, and eyewear correctly rated for your device's wavelength before they'll approve you; many expect sign-off from a Laser Protection Adviser. Trading without the licence is an offence — a June 2026 prosecution resulted in a fine plus costs.

**Outside London.** Most councils have no equivalent scheme for laser/IPL — it falls outside the skin-piercing registration most areas use for tattooing and microneedling. That's not a green light: ring your environmental health team and get their answer in writing, because a handful of councils run their own local rules.

**Scotland.** The Non-Surgical Procedures and Functions of Medical Reviewers (Scotland) Act 2026 passed this year and names laser hair removal as a licensable procedure. The licensing scheme itself isn't live yet — check with your council on commencement before assuming either way.

**England-wide.** The Health and Care Act 2022 gives government the power to create a national licensing scheme covering procedures like this. It still hasn't been switched on.

Your insurer's policy wording is the other half of this — a licence doesn't guarantee cover, and cover doesn't replace a licence. Check both, and file the answer alongside your [council licence check for microneedling and SPMU](https://say-salon.com/blog/do-i-need-a-council-licence-for-microneedling-or-spmu-uk).
