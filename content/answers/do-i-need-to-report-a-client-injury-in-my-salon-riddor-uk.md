# Do I need to report a client injury in my salon?

Sometimes, but far less often than most owners fear. Whether you must tell the Health and Safety Executive (HSE) depends on who was hurt and how badly, under RIDDOR (the Reporting of Injuries, Diseases and Dangerous Occurrences Regulations 2013).

**Clients and other members of the public** are only reportable if the accident arose out of your work activity *and* the person was taken from the premises straight to hospital for treatment. A burn from a wax pot or a client who faints and is sent to A&E can qualify. A graze, a mild reaction, or a client who later chooses to see their GP does not.

**Staff** are reportable for deaths, "specified injuries" (such as fractures, other than fingers and toes, or serious burns), and injuries that stop them doing their normal work for more than seven consecutive days. The over-seven-day injuries must be reported within 15 days. Deaths and specified injuries must be reported without delay.

Reports are made online through the HSE's RIDDOR portal. Self-employed chair renters should check their own position, as they are responsible for their own reporting.

Whatever the threshold, record every incident:

- Date, time and place, and the treatment being carried out
- What happened and what the person said at the time
- First aid given and whether anyone attended hospital
- Who witnessed it, and any follow-up contact with the client

Keep an accident book. It is a legal requirement for employers with ten or more employees under the Social Security regulations, and sensible for every salon. Store entries so personal details aren't visible to the next person who opens the book, and keep records for at least three years. Photographs of a reaction or burn, with the client's consent, can help later.

Finally, tell your insurer promptly, even if the client says they are fine. Most policies require early notification, and a late report can put a claim at risk.

*This is general guidance, not legal advice. Check the HSE's current RIDDOR guidance for your circumstances.*
