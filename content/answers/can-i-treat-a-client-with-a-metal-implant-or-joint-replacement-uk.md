# Can I treat a client with a metal implant or joint replacement?

Usually yes — but the answer depends entirely on which treatment, and on how close the metal sits to it.

**Radiofrequency and microcurrent: avoid the site, not the client.** RF generates an electromagnetic field and metal conducts heat, so nearly every device manufacturer contraindicates treating directly over a plate, pin, screw, or prosthetic joint. The same logic applies to microcurrent, EMS and galvanic. A knee replacement doesn't stop you doing an RF facial. A titanium plate in the jaw does stop you running RF along that jawline. Check the manufacturer's instructions for use — that document, not a forum post, is what your insurer will hold you to.

**Laser and IPL: generally irrelevant.** Light energy is absorbed in the skin and doesn't reach a plate sitting under muscle or bone. What does matter is metal on the surface: remove piercings from the treatment area, and never fire over surgical staples or clips.

**Massage: check the timeline, not the metal.** A healed hip or knee replacement is fine. Avoid deep pressure directly over the joint, and treat anything still within the surgeon's recovery window as [recent surgery](https://say-salon.com/blog/can-i-treat-a-client-after-recent-surgery-or-over-a-new-scar-uk).

What to do:

- **Ask where the metal is, not just whether there is any.** "Hip replacement, left side, 2019" is usable. "Yes" is not.
- **Record it as permanent.** Implants don't expire; the flag must still be there when a different therapist books RF in two years.
- **Cardiac devices are a separate question** — a pacemaker or ICD is an [absolute contraindication to electrical treatments](https://say-salon.com/blog/can-i-use-microcurrent-or-radiofrequency-on-a-client-with-a-pacemaker-uk), not a site-avoidance one.
