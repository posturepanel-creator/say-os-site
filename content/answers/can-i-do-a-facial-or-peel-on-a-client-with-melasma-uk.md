# Can I do a facial or peel on a client with melasma?

Short answer: yes to gentle facials, but be cautious with anything that heats, irritates or inflames the skin. Melasma is a pigmentation condition driven by hormones, UV and heat. It is not infectious, but aggressive treatment commonly makes it darker and can trigger post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation.

**Usually fine:**

- Gentle cleansing, hydrating and barrier-supporting facials
- Light enzyme or low-strength lactic or mandelic acid exfoliation, used cautiously
- Cool, soothing massage and masks

**Avoid or refer on:**

- Strong peels (high-strength glycolic, TCA, phenol)
- Laser, IPL and radiofrequency, because heat can worsen melasma
- Microneedling and dermaplaning without medical guidance
- Hot towels, steam and heated masks

**What to do at consultation:**

- Ask whether the melasma has been diagnosed, and whether the client is on prescribed treatments such as hydroquinone or tretinoin. Those thin the skin and raise irritation risk.
- Record triggers: the contraceptive pill, HRT, pregnancy, sun exposure.
- Patch test any new active on a discreet area and wait at least 48 hours before treating the face.
- Photograph the skin with consent so you can show it hasn't been made worse.

**Aftercare matters as much as the treatment.** Daily broad-spectrum SPF 50 is non-negotiable, as melasma returns quickly with UV exposure, even indoors near windows. If the client is unsure whether it is melasma or something else, such as a changing mole, refer them to a GP or dermatologist before treating.

Record the consultation, the advice given and the client's consent on their card.
