# Do I need a council licence for microneedling or semi-permanent makeup in the UK?

Almost certainly yes — and which licence you need depends on where you trade, not on what you call the treatment. There is no single UK-wide scheme, which is why so many salons get this wrong.

**England, outside London.** Most councils register premises and practitioners for skin-piercing activities — tattooing, semi-permanent makeup, electrolysis, acupuncture — under the Local Government (Miscellaneous Provisions) Act 1982. Some councils treat microneedling as skin piercing; some don't. Ring environmental health and ask about your specific device and depth.

**London boroughs.** The London Local Authorities Act 1991 adds a "special treatments" licence covering a much wider list, including many facial, laser and needling treatments. It's annual, costs considerably more, and carries premises standards and inspection.

**Wales.** A mandatory national licensing scheme for special procedures under the Public Health (Wales) Act 2017 — a practitioner licence plus approved premises.

**Scotland.** Licensing sits with each council under the Civic Government (Scotland) Act 1982; skin piercing and tattooing are routinely covered.

**Northern Ireland.** District councils register skin-piercing premises and operators. Check with yours directly.

Three things that catch people out:

- Trading without the licence your council requires is a criminal offence, and your insurer may decline a claim off the back of it.
- Your insurance policy — not the licence — is usually what actually limits which treatments you can offer. Check both, separately.
- England has powers under the Health and Care Act 2022 to create a national licensing scheme for non-surgical cosmetic procedures. It has not been brought into force. Keep an eye on it.

Get your council's answer in writing and file it with your [consultation records](https://say-salon.com/blog/how-long-should-i-keep-client-consultation-records-uk).
