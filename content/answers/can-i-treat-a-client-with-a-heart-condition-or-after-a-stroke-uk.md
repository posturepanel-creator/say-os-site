# Can I treat a client with a heart condition or who has had a stroke?

Often yes — but a diagnosed heart condition or a past stroke is a different conversation from ordinary [high blood pressure](/blog/can-i-massage-a-client-with-high-blood-pressure-uk). The question isn't whether they have a heart history. It's how recent the event was, whether they're stable, and what your treatment does to circulation, heat load and blood pressure.

Why it matters: heat treatments, deep massage, full-body wraps and lymphatic work all shift fluid and change circulatory demand. In a client with angina, heart failure, an arrhythmia, or a recent cardiac event or stroke, that shift can provoke symptoms on your couch. Many will also be on anticoagulants, which brings [bruising and bleeding risk](/blog/can-i-wax-or-do-microneedling-on-a-client-taking-blood-thinners-uk) into waxing, microneedling and firm massage.

What to do:

- **Get the timeline.** A stroke, heart attack, stent or cardiac surgery within the last 6 months means GP or consultant clearance before anything beyond a gentle facial or manicure.
- **Ask what they're on.** Anticoagulants, beta blockers, GTN spray. Record it on the consultation form and ask them to bring the spray to the appointment.
- **Avoid heat overload.** No sauna, steam, hot stones, heated wraps or prolonged infrared for unstable cardiac clients.
- **Keep massage gentle and short.** Skip deep tissue and vigorous lymphatic drainage.
- **Watch for post-stroke changes.** Reduced sensation on one side means they may not feel wax, heat or pressure that is burning them. Test temperature yourself, every time.
- **Sit them up slowly** and keep the room cool.

If they can't tell you clearly what their condition is or when it happened, that uncertainty is your answer: pause and get written clearance. Document the decision either way — a declined treatment you recorded is a defence; one you didn't is a gap.
