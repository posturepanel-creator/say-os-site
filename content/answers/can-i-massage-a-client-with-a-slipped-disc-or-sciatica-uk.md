# Can I massage a client with a slipped disc or sciatica?

Sometimes, but not on the day the pain is acute, and not without checking first. A slipped (prolapsed) disc or sciatica is a local contraindication at best and a medical referral at worst, depending on what the client tells you at consultation.

**Do not treat, and refer to their GP or NHS 111, if the client reports:**

- New or worsening numbness, tingling or weakness in the legs or feet.
- Pain that is severe, constant or worse at night.
- Loss of bladder or bowel control, or numbness around the genitals or buttocks — this can signal cauda equina syndrome and needs **emergency care (999 or A&E)**, not a massage.
- Back pain following a fall or accident, or alongside fever or unexplained weight loss.

**If the condition is diagnosed and settled, you can usually proceed with adaptations:**

- Avoid deep pressure, percussion and stretching directly over the spine and the affected nerve path.
- Work on the surrounding muscles (glutes, hips, hamstrings) with light to moderate pressure, and stop if symptoms spread or sharpen.
- Position carefully — lying flat on the front can aggravate a disc, so use side-lying or bolsters.
- Ask whether a physiotherapist or doctor has advised against massage, and follow that advice.

**Record it:** note the diagnosis, what the client reported, any GP or physio advice, the adaptations you made, and their consent on the consultation form. If you are ever unsure, a short "not today, please check with your GP" protects the client and your insurance position. Always check your own insurer's terms and your training provider's guidance.
