How should I handle last-minute cancellations?

A last-minute cancellation hurts more than a no-show in one way: you usually find out too late to fill the slot. The goal is to shift cancellations earlier and have a system ready to backfill when they happen.

Set a clear cancellation window and state it at booking — 24 or 48 hours is standard in UK salons. Clients respect a policy they were told about upfront; they resent one sprung on them afterward. Put it in the booking confirmation, not the small print.

Build a waitlist. Most last-minute gaps can be filled if you can reach the right person fast. Keep a list of clients who wanted an earlier slot, and when one opens up, offer it to them automatically. A gap filled in twenty minutes is a gap that never cost you anything.

Distinguish the genuine emergency from the habit. Life happens — a one-off cancellation from a loyal client is just goodwill spent well. But when the same client cancels inside the window repeatedly, that's a pattern worth seeing. Quietly tracking it lets you decide who to ask for a deposit next time, without policing everyone.

Make the early cancel easier than the late one. If a client can cancel in two taps the moment their plans change, they'll do it at 9am instead of 5pm — and that's the difference between a slot you can refill and one you can't.
