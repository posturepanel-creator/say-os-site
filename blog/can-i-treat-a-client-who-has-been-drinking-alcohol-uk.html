Safety & Contraindications

Can I treat a client who has been drinking alcohol?

Usually no — and unlike most contraindications, this one is squarely your call to make. There is no protected characteristic in being drunk on the day, so declining carries none of the discrimination risk that refusing a disabled or pregnant client would.

Two separate problems stack up.

Consent. Intoxication affects capacity the same way any temporary impairment does: it is decision-specific and time-specific. If a client can't take in what you're explaining, weigh it up, and give you a coherent answer, you do not have informed consent — and a signature scrawled on a consultation form does not fix that. A patch-test declaration or medical-history form completed while drunk is worthless evidence if a reaction follows.

Clinical risk. Alcohol is a vasodilator and mildly anticoagulant:

  • Waxing, microneedling, threading — flushed, engorged skin bruises and bleeds more, and lifts more easily.
  • Massage, heat treatments, saunas — vasodilation plus dehydration raises the chance of fainting mid-treatment.
  • Chemical peels, laser and IPL — expect heightened erythema and a less predictable endpoint.
  • Hair colour and lash tint — the chemistry is unaffected, but the client cannot reliably report a stinging scalp or a burning eye, which is your early-warning system for a reaction.
  • Nails and dry cutting — generally fine if they can sit still and follow instruction.

Check your policy wording. Most treatment-risk insurers exclude claims where the client was visibly under the influence, so proceeding can leave you personally exposed even if the treatment goes well.

Rebook rather than refuse outright. Note in the record that treatment was deferred and why — pre-wedding and hen-party bookings are where this comes up, and a dated note protects you if the complaint arrives later.
