Safety & Contraindications

Which salon treatments should I avoid on a pregnant client?

Most salon treatments are fine during pregnancy, but a handful aren't — and the safest position is to ask, then adjust, rather than refuse everything or carry on as normal. Pregnancy changes circulation, skin sensitivity, and pigmentation, so a treatment that was routine three months ago can react differently now.

What UK insurers and training bodies generally flag:

  • Chemical peels and strong actives: avoid high-strength acids and anything containing retinoids. Skin is more reactive and prone to pigmentation, and most peels aren't tested for use in pregnancy.
  • Electrical treatments (galvanic, microcurrent, electrolysis, some IPL/laser): commonly contraindicated, especially in the first trimester. Check your insurer's wording — many require GP sign-off or decline cover outright.
  • Heat treatments (saunas, hot stones, heated wraps): raising core temperature is the concern; keep it gentle.
  • Essential oils in aromatherapy: several are contraindicated in pregnancy, so a standard blend isn't automatically safe.

Generally fine with care: facials, manicures, pedicures, and waxing — though skin is more sensitive, so patch awareness and gentler technique matter.

The thing that catches salons out is the first trimester, when a client may not have told you she's pregnant. Your consultation form has to ask directly, and the answer needs to be dated and on record before you start — not remembered from a chat at the basin.
