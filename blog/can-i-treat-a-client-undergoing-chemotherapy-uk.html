Safety & Contraindications

Can I treat a client who is undergoing chemotherapy?

Sometimes — but almost never on your judgement alone. Most UK insurers and training bodies treat active cancer treatment as a *medical-referral* contraindication: you can treat, but only with written consent from the client's GP or oncology team, and often only if you hold specific oncology training. Treating without that consent can void your insurance entirely.

Why the caution is real, not box-ticking:

  • Skin changes. Chemotherapy makes skin thin, dry, and reactive. Products the client tolerated for years can now burn or blister. Re-do every patch test — old results no longer count.
  • Infection risk. Immunity is suppressed, so hygiene lapses that a healthy client would shrug off can put this client in hospital.
  • Ports and PICC lines. Never massage or apply pressure near a chemo port or line site. Ask where it is before any body treatment.
  • Neuropathy. Numb hands and feet mean the client can't feel if water, wax, or stones are too hot. Your thermometer becomes their nervous system.
  • Hair colour. Wait until roughly 6 months of regrowth after treatment ends, then patch test again — the hair and scalp that grow back are not the ones you knew.
