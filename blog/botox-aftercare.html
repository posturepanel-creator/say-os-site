Botox Aftercare: The Do's and Don'ts That Protect Your Results

Most botox aftercare rules come down to a single fact: for the first day or so, the toxin can still move. Here's what to do, what to skip, and why.

Published August 2026 · 6 min read · General information, not medical advice

The result of a botox appointment isn't settled the moment you leave the chair. Botulinum toxin takes days to bind to the nerve endings it's meant to relax — and in that window, what you do with your face matters. Good botox aftercare is mostly about protecting a treatment that hasn't finished working yet.

The reassuring part: the rules are few, they're short, and once you understand the one mechanism behind them, they're easy to remember.

The one rule behind all the rules

Botox is injected as a liquid. In the first hours it can still spread slightly from where it was placed — Cleveland Clinic puts it plainly: "Botox is liquid, and liquid can spread, although only by about half an inch." That's the whole reason aftercare exists. Rubbing, pressure, and anything that raises blood flow to the area can nudge the product toward muscles it wasn't meant to reach — which is how you get uneven results or, rarely, a temporarily heavy brow or eyelid.

So almost every "don't" below is really the same instruction: for the first day, don't move the product.

The first 4 hours

Stay upright

Keep upright for about 4 hours — no lying down or face-planting on the sofa. Staying vertical helps the product settle where it was placed.

Don't rub or press the area

No rubbing, massaging or firm face-washing over the injection sites. Gentle cleansing is fine; pressure is not.

Go easy on makeup

Let the tiny injection points close first (roughly 15 minutes to an hour). Mineral makeup applied without pressure is the gentlest option.

Normal expressions are fine

Talking, smiling and blinking don't undo anything — it's external pressure and heat you're avoiding, not your own face.

The first 24 hours

Once you're past the first few hours, a handful of things stay on the list for a full day — mostly because they raise blood pressure, blood flow or bruising risk while everything settles.

No strenuous exercise

Skip the gym, running and hot yoga for 24 hours. Exercise raises blood pressure, which can increase bruising and swelling.

No alcohol

Alcohol thins the blood and makes bruising more likely — leave it for 24 hours (and ideally the day before, too).

No facials, massage or heat

No facials or facial massage for 24 hours, and many practitioners also advise skipping saunas, sunbeds and very hot showers, which raise blood flow to the face.

Watch the painkillers

Aspirin and anti-inflammatories like ibuprofen can add to bruising on the day. Paracetamol is usually the gentler choice — but follow your practitioner's advice.

The one nobody expects: skip the motorbike

It's genuinely not advised to ride a motorbike straight after botox — a snug helmet presses on the forehead, and steady pressure in the first day or two can push the product off-target. Tight hats, visors and headbands are out for the same reason, for roughly 24–48 hours. If your treatment was in the forehead or frown lines, give the crash helmet a rest until tomorrow.

When it actually works — and how long it lasts

Botox doesn't work instantly, so don't judge it in the mirror on day one. Across clinical and practitioner guidance the timeline is consistent:

First signs: 3–5 days

You'll usually notice movement softening within a few days of treatment.

Full effect: 10–14 days

The result matures over about two weeks — wait the full 14 days before judging it, as small asymmetries often even out.

Lasts: 3–4 months

Effects typically hold for around 12–16 weeks before movement gradually returns.

Because of that two-week window, the smart move is to book a review at around day 14 rather than panicking on day two — and to keep a note of the date, so you know when a top-up is genuinely due rather than guessing.

Where this quietly goes wrong

None of these rules are hard. The problem is remembering them a week later — which product went where, how many units, when the review is due, what your last practitioner told you to avoid. For most people that lives on a crumpled appointment card or nowhere at all.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long after botox can I lie down?

Stay upright for about four hours after treatment. Lying down too soon can let the product migrate away from the injection sites.

Can I exercise after botox?

Avoid strenuous exercise for 24 hours. Raising your blood pressure in the first day can increase bruising and swelling; light walking is generally fine after the first few hours.

Can I wear a helmet or a hat after botox?

Avoid tight helmets, hats, visors and headbands for roughly 24–48 hours, especially after forehead or frown-line treatment. Steady pressure on the area can move the product before it settles — which is why riding a motorbike straight after isn't advised.

How long does botox take to work?

Most people see the first effects within 3–5 days, with the full result at about 10–14 days. Wait the full two weeks before judging it.

How long does botox last?

Typically 3–4 months (around 12–16 weeks), after which muscle movement gradually returns and a top-up may be considered.

Can I drink alcohol after botox?

Leave alcohol for 24 hours. It thins the blood and makes bruising at the injection sites more likely.

A note on this guide. This is general information, not medical advice. Botulinum toxin is a prescription-only medicine that should be administered by a qualified, regulated practitioner, and their specific aftercare instructions always take priority over anything here. If you have unusual pain, spreading weakness, drooping or signs of infection, contact your practitioner or a doctor.
